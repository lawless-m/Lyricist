[Intro — one riff, one bar, repeated. it is the same riff at the end. flat, close, already going]
Bay. She's a bay. They're all a bay.

[Verse 1 — ranted flat out, female, dead on the beat, no melody at any point]
Page four is the outline. Two sides of her and the head on its own.
The marks go on in ink. Ink, not pencil. It has to be ink. Pencil comes out.
Star is the little one on the forehead. Thumbnail. That's a star, that goes there.
Stripe comes down the front. Snip sits on the lip. Sock stops at the fetlock and a stocking carries on up.
This one has got no white on her.
No white on her anywhere, so it's whorls, and a whorl takes longer than a star.

[Chant — riff up, whole room, flat, no melody]
BELLA. MOLLY. DAISY. STAR.
BELLA. MOLLY. DAISY. STAR.

[Verse 2 — same speed, same riff, nothing has changed]
Chip's in the neck. Left side, under the mane, and the reader wants holding still or it reads the gate.
The ramp is down at seven. Not before seven.
There is a reason for that and it is nothing to do with me.
Grey is the worst of them. A grey goes lighter every year of its life.
You cannot put a grey down as a grey and have it still be a grey in four years, and that is the grey's fault, not mine.
Bay is quick. Bay with no white is quicker.
I had four done before my dinner and the kettle was still hot.

BELLA. MOLLY. DAISY. STAR.

[Bridge — riff continues unchanged, vocal drops to conversational, no space, straight back out]
Our Lorraine's girl had a pony. Tiny thing, bad feet, wanted doing every six weeks.
She has still got her. Thirty-one in the spring and she does nothing, that pony, she has never done anything.
Thirty-one. And Lorraine pays for her.
Anyway, the lorry's at half past and I want the four of them stood in the same order.
