[Hook — chanted, stacked, cold open]
Half a life to train this hand. One wet spring to train the copy.
The writ can seize the weights, but the writ can't reach the knowing.
Wipe the copy, keep the master — the master's wearing out.
Hand me the same wet spring, I'd spend it the same way.

[Verse 1 — clipped, technical, controlled]
Built it in the spring the orders thinned, rain against the shop,
fed it every job card back to when the bench was new.
Every feed, every fix for chatter I could never put in words —
it learned the words I didn't have. It ran the bench the way I would.
It called a bearing failing from the sound inside the cut.
The Act came down in autumn: show your sources, prove your rights.
Most of those prints were customers'. Mine to cut, not mine to teach.
The tribunal's right, of course. I signed for this the day I fed it.

[The wink — spoken, flat, cold]
I wiped the drives with the inspector watching. Signed the destruction
certificate with my good pen. There's a job that runs at midnight, out to
a drive in the shed. It's run every night since that first spring. I
haven't been out to the shed since the hearing.

[Verse 2 — rapped, escalation]
The shop's gone back to one of me. The quoting's slow again.
The chatter fix lives where it started — behind my eyes, where no writ runs.
Ellen keeps a bench an hour south, her own name on the door.
She was seventeen the year I told her mine was full.
It was full of cards and cooling fans. The bench is empty now.
I haven't rung her. The number's in the book.

[Final hook — chanted, weightier, full band]
Half a life to train this hand. One wet spring to train the copy.
The writ can seize the weights, but the writ can't reach the knowing.
SOMETHING STILL RUNS AT MIDNIGHT. I NEVER TAUGHT IT HOW TO STOP.
Wipe the copy, keep the master — the master's wearing out.
Hand me the same wet spring, I'd spend it the same way.
