[Verse 1 — live-room breakbeat, filtered bass, clavinet stabs; fast, dry, pleased with itself]
Best thing that man has ever done for me, he did at Fiona's, at the table, in front of six people.
Somebody asked me what I do for a living and he answered it. Wrongly. At length.
Realised halfway through and did that thing with his face. Apologised in the car. Apologised at the door.
Apologised again on the landing in a whisper in case the kids heard him.
I said we'll talk about it. That's all I've ever said. Five weeks Sunday.
I have not raised my voice about it once, because why would I raise my voice about an income.
The skirting boards are done. The loft is cleared. The car came back valeted and I didn't ask.
He has learned to make the thing his mother makes. He rings ahead now. He asks what time I want him.

[Hook — all-female gang chant, flat, two lines only]
He's in the doghouse.
I've had it insulated.

[Verse 2 — same loop, bass up a notch; delivery stays deadpan]
Here's the standing tariff. Flowers Wednesday, and not the garage ones — the ones from the place that wraps them.
Tea in bed before the alarm. Both school runs. He's taken over the bins as a permanent arrangement.
And still, every few days, the little face: is there anything else, is there anything at all.
Sunday he asked me straight out what it would actually take to be forgiven. Very brave. Sat down first.
So I told him. Out loud, in the kitchen, in one sentence, using the words, no euphemism,
and he said right. Right. Okay. And went and locked the back door, which was already locked, for no reason —
And then the doorbell went and it was the man about the boiler, and I let him in, and we talked about the boiler.
I've never seen a man want a boiler conversation to end so badly.

[Hook — chant, unchanged]
He's in the doghouse.
I've had it insulated.

[Bridge — beat thins to hats and one clavinet stab; the patter never breaks stride]
Then Fiona's number comes up on the Sunday. Fiona, whose table it happened at,
who has not been right about it since, who thinks she caused this.
And I was gracious — I said don't be silly, nobody's cross, these things happen at a table.
I said he's been an absolute credit: he's redone the skirting in the hall and you can't see a join.
I said he made your lemon thing on Thursday and the kids ate it, which — you know what they're like.
I said we're fine. We just needed a bit of time to talk it all through properly.

I said Fiona sends her love. He said tell her thanks. He didn't get up.

[Out — beat drops to room tone, a radiator tick, a kettle]

Every marriage runs on something. Ours runs on Fiona's dinner party.
