[Verse 1 — live-room breakbeat, filtered bass, dusty horn-section stabs; voice close-mic'd, dry, fast, pleased with itself]
The showroom opens at half past eight; I'm on the floor by eight,
walking the stock with a coffee: what's aged, what's priced to move.
My name's been top of the board so long they've stopped updating the board.
I could sell the courtesy car to a man who came in for a service.

Finance, extras, trade-ins — I know the book value of anything you drive in,
and roughly what you'll take for it once you've seen me lean across the bonnet.
The suit is charcoal, the skirt is a pencil, the heels are within policy.
There's a reason this branch's test-drive routes are longer than the other branches'.

[Hook — all-female gang chant, flat, two lines only]
Plenty of satisfaction.
It's guaranteed.

[Verse 2 — same loop, bass up a notch; delivery stays deadpan]
Saturday, ten o'clock: he books the flagship for a test drive —
the one he's been circling online for a month like it might notice him.
I take the first stretch, to demonstrate the parking assist. We swap.
He adjusts the mirror three times. Then he stalls it. It's an automatic.

I said take the B-road, that's where she shows you what she's got.
I said torque like it was a secret between the three of us.
He went for the gearstick and found the handbrake. We were already parked —
there's a lay-by on the coast road with a view the brochure doesn't mention.
The brochure doesn't mention a lot of things.

[Hook — chant, unchanged]
Plenty of satisfaction.
It's guaranteed.

[Bridge — beat thins to hats and one muted horn; the patter never breaks stride]
The sales manager, when we got back: four hours? Four? For a test drive?
And I was fluent: I said you cannot rush a considered purchase.
I said we did the full route — town, motorway, the coast for the ride quality.
I said he had a great many questions about the finance, and I answered every single one.
I said this is how you build a customer for life, Trevor. Relationships.
He looked at the odometer. He looked at me. He signed the fuel log.
People don't buy the car, they buy the experience. I've said it at conference.

[Out — beat drops to room tone, a forecourt tannoy]
The valet had the demonstrator back on the forecourt in ten minutes. Nothing to do.
The paperwork took twenty: top spec, paint protection, the mats, the extended everything.
His satisfaction survey came back five stars, every box,
and under "anything we could improve?" he wrote "no," and underlined it.
Salesperson of the quarter. Again. The plaque wall's getting crowded.
He's back on Saturday. He's bringing his brother.

[Final hook — chant, once, dry]
Plenty of satisfaction.
It's guaranteed.
