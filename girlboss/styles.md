Late-90s / early-00s indie hip-hop. Live-sounding breakbeat, filtered bass, and one cheap stab texture rotated per song — clavinet, Rhodes, dusty horn-section hits, scratched vinyl stabs, a chopped soul sample, wah guitar, cheap harpsichord. (The list is meant to grow — when every texture on it has been used recently, add one that suits the song's world rather than repeating the freshest; harpsichord was added for the finishing school.) (Organ was the default long enough to wear out; don't let any single texture become the new organ.) Keep the hard qualifier on whichever texture is up — *cheap* clavinet stabs, *dirty* wah guitar stabs, *cheap* scratched-vinyl stabs. A bare texture name drifts soft: "scratched vinyl stabs" on its own came back as wow-and-flutter tremolo with the edge gone. Female vocal, conversational-to-fast, close-mic'd, dry — the voice sits in front of the beat, not in it. Hook can be all-female-gang-chanted or doubled. Nothing polished; the record should sound like it cost very little and knew exactly what it was doing.

**Start from the reference prompt.** `you'd-better-be` is the one to copy — it is the proven skeleton and the whole term order matters:

```
late-90s indie hip-hop, live-sounding breakbeat, filtered bass, cheap organ stabs, fast conversational female vocal, close-mic'd and dry, voice in front of the beat, all-female gang-chanted hook, no sung chorus, lo-fi, unpolished, deadpan, self-assured
```

Rotate the texture, swap the attitude word, and otherwise leave it alone.

**Instruct the delivery positively — don't just forbid the wrong one.** "No sung chorus" was the only anti-singing term for the first batch and it stopped holding: August takes came back sung and melodic off prompts that had landed flat in July. A lone negative is the weakest instruction in the field. So keep `fast conversational female vocal` and `no sung chorus` where they are, and add the positive form straight after the vocal:

```
rapped and spoken
```

**A little singing is allowed and can be the nice change — it just has to be chosen.** The failure mode isn't a sung line, it's a whole take drifting melodic and losing the edge. So don't ban it in the style field; decide per song which one section lifts, and say so in that section's tag — `[Hook — sung, one line only, then back to flat]`, `[Out — half-sung, dry]`. Everywhere else stays spoken. If a song wants none at all, `no singing anywhere` is available as a style-field term, but treat that as this song's decision rather than the house default.

Back it up in the lyric itself: **every section tag carries its own delivery instruction**, the way laundry's do — `[Verse 1 — spoken, fast, conversational, dry, flat, no melody]`, `[Hook — flat gang chant, shouted not sung, two lines]`, `[Bridge — rapid spoken count, no melody, breakbeat only]`. Attitude words in a tag ("courteous", "pleased with itself", "the patter never breaks stride") describe *character* and do nothing to stop a melody arriving; say spoken and say no melody. Structural instructions belong in the tag of the section they govern, never in the style field — "chanted cold a cappella before the beat drops" sat at the front of one prompt and told the whole record to go beatless.

Close on one attitude word per song, and make it an edge word — sly, cocky, proprietorial, brazen, unhurried. Smooth ones (impeccable) sand the record down.
