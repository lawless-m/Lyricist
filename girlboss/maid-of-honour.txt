[Verse 1 — live-room breakbeat, filtered bass, cheap organ stab; voice close-mic'd, dry, fast, delighted with itself]
Six months of spreadsheets — I planned this like a moon landing.
I've got the rings, the receipts, I've got the vicar on speed dial.
She said "you're the only one I trust with the whole of today,"
and she's right. Nothing happens today unless it goes through me.

I zipped her into the dress myself — some jobs want a professional.
I swept the seating plan for exes and confiscated three.
I know this couple better than they'll ever know each other:
I've read the vows. Both sets. I made a few improvements.

[Hook — all-female gang chant, flat, two lines only]
Anything you need today.
Anything at all.

[Verse 2 — same loop, bass up a notch; delivery stays deadpan]
Half twelve: found the groom in the corridor by the vestry,
grey as his morning suit, cufflinks in the wrong holes.
He asked me was it normal that the floor's at a slight angle.
I fixed his cufflinks. Slowly. I straightened a buttonhole with nothing wrong with it.

And I said: one for the road, love. No card, no name on it.
Last blowjob of your single life — going once. Going—
[the beat cuts for one bar]
My phone lights up. It's her: "where are you, I can't breathe."
Duty calls. I left him holding the pause. He'll be holding it for years.

[Hook — chant, unchanged]
Anything you need today.
Anything at all.

[Bridge — beat thins to hats and organ; the patter never breaks stride]
Two o'clock, make-up room, she's in the robe, in the chair,
saying "tell me honestly — am I doing the right thing?"
And I'm fluent: I said he wept when he read your vows this morning.
I said he's calm as a lake out there. I said he hasn't stopped smiling.
Then I take her face in both my hands like I'm checking the contour,
and I kiss her. On the mouth. Once. Professionally.
"You look like everything he doesn't deserve. Now blot."
And she laughs, and she breathes, and she marries him at three,
wearing my nerve on her lips under two coats of Pillow Talk.

[Out — beat drops to room noise, a spoon on a glass]
Eight o'clock, I stood up and I gave the toast of the century.
I said marriage is built on trust — and nobody knows that better than me.
Her mother cried. His mother cried. He studied the tablecloth.
They've asked me to godmother the first one.
I said: anything at all.
