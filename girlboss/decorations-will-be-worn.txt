[Intro — no beat, all-female gang chant, cold, read off a card, spoken flat, no melody]
Dress: black tie.
Decorations will be worn.

[Verse 1 — live-room breakbeat drops in, filtered bass, scratched vinyl stabs; voice close-mic'd, dry, fast, spoken, no melody, pleased with itself]
Our firm's been cutting for this mess since before the mess had a mess to sit in.
My father's name is over the door. The back room is mine, and the back room is where it gets decided.
I have every man at this dinner in a ledger — chest, waist, sleeve, seat —
and the year each one of them stopped being that number and asked me, very quietly, not to write it down.

Barathea, doeskin, a collar that sits or the whole thing's a bin bag with medals on it.
Overalls strapped under the boot. You can tell a bought man from a fitted man across a car park.
And a tailor doesn't talk. That's the trade. Four generations of drink and fittings and we have never talked.
I got there at seven. I touched one collar. It was crooked and it was killing me.

[Hook — all-female gang chant, shouted not sung, flat, two lines only]
Dress: black tie.
Decorations will be worn.

[Verse 2 — same loop, bass up a notch; delivery stays courteous, spoken, no melody]
Now. The dress. I cut it myself, on the table, shop shut, no pattern, one afternoon —
scarlet, off the same bolt as the stripe that runs down every leg in this room.
So they are all wearing a piece of it, and not one of them has worked that out,
and I'm not going to be the one who tells them, and neither, I think, are you.

Somebody's subaltern — twenty-three, three drinks in, feeling gallant — said this dress should be a war crime.
I said it isn't a war crime, sweetheart, it's a size ten, and there's more work in your jacket than in mine.
Which there is. Which is the problem. Because he is a forty-two long standing in a forty-four,
and I lost the whole thread of the evening looking at what that was doing to his shoulders.

[Verse 2, second half — hats drop out under the first line, come back in on "toast"]
The Adjutant runs this dinner to the minute, and he's sat up there in his father's jacket.
I let that jacket out myself, years back, when I was still on the machine. There is nothing left in the seams.
He knows that I know that. He has known it all night. He has been sat very straight all night.
Ninety men stand for the loyal toast, and he stands, and he lifts the glass, and it goes across the back.

Not a pop. A long one. You could hear it from where I was and I was three tables off.
He drank the toast with it open, sat down on it, and kept his arms at his sides through the speeches and the door.
[the beat cuts for one bar]
So I said: that seam is mine, that's my work gone, and I'm not doing it here in a dining room.
Come to the shop Monday. We're shut Mondays. Bring the jacket.

[Hook — chant, unchanged, shouted not sung]
Dress: black tie.
Decorations will be worn.

[Bridge — beat thins to hats and one vinyl stab; the patter never breaks stride, spoken, no melody]
Half eleven the phone goes and it's the guv'nor. Eighty-one, can't sleep, wants the night's numbers.
So he gets them. All of them. Six new orders, two re-cuts, a waistcoat for a Colonel
who has not changed shape since decimalisation and is extremely proud of it.
Told him the young ones are all coming in off their fathers' jackets and we ought to be quoting them new,
and to get the barathea in before the mills shut for August or we'll be apologising until Christmas.
He wrote it down. I could hear him writing it down. He asked me, was it a good room.
It was a lovely room, I said, and they'd been very kind about the firm, and I'd got the cards in.
I was stood in the corridor by the cloakroom with somebody's mess jacket over my arm for all of that,
and I never put it down.

[Out — beat drops to room tone, a chair scraping, a hoover somewhere; spoken, flat]
There's a fresh set of numbers against his name in the ledger, dated the Saturday.
Neck, chest, waist, sleeve. In pencil, in my hand, same as every other entry in that book.
Nobody gets measured at a dinner.
You can always tell the ones I've done, you know. They stand like they're being looked at.
I put that in. I charge for it.
The Mess Secretary has written to us — says the mess has not looked like that on parade in thirty years.
They want us for the summer ball, and the Colonel wants a word about the waistcoat,
and he'd like me to come out and do it at his house, personally, rather than have it done in the shop.

[Final hook — chant, once, dry, shouted not sung]
Dress: black tie.
Decorations will be worn.
