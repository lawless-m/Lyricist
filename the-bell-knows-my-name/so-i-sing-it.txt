[Verse 1 — slow, intimate, solo violin, rubato]
Birds don't sing because they're happy. They sing because it's the only thing they've got.
Give one a word — one word, the right one — and you would not hear another note out of it.
I have been paid to sing at every wedding in this valley for thirty years.
Everybody knows what's in it. It does not need saying, and it does not get said.

[Pre-chorus — building, accordion swells in]
and the fiddle says the whole of it
and nobody asks it to explain

[Chorus — full band]
so take it high, take it wordless, let the room do what a room does —
give a bird a word and you would never hear it sing again.
I have had the word for thirty years, and you have had the song.
Would you rather I had said it?

[Verse 2 — slow again]
Mirela was at the front table in September, with her husband and her sons.
I sang the one about the orchard. I sing the one about the orchard at all of them.
She looked down at the cloth for the middle part, and then she looked up again.
Then she danced with her husband, who is a good man, and I played for that as well.

[Chorus — full band]
so take it high, take it wordless, let the room do what a room does —
give a bird a word and you would never hear it sing again.
I have had the word for thirty years, and you have had the song.
Would you rather I had said it?

[Bridge — half-time, sparse, then huge]
maybe a word was never braver
maybe a word was just the end
maybe the reason it still holds
is neither of us has to answer
— (violin builds, tempo doubles) —
so let it staaay—

[Final Chorus — biggest, gang vocals on the last line]
take it high, take it wordless, let the room do what a room does —
give a bird a word and you would never hear it sing again.
I have had the word since before your eldest, and you have had the song,
and you know every note of it, and not one of them is mine to say.
(gang) so I sing it.
so I sing it.
