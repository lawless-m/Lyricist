[Verse 1 — slow, intimate, solo violin, rubato]
Rada took me in at four. I don't know from where. It was not discussed.
I learned the yard before my letters, and I left the letters be.
I can name you every horse that came through here in thirty years —
what it feared, what it favoured, and which side it liked the wall.

[Pre-chorus — building, accordion swells in]
and the fiddle comes when I call it
the way the horses always did

[Chorus — full band]
so walk him out, walk him sound, let them see him moving clean —
a dog born in a stable is a dog, and they were right to say it.
I can shoe near anything that breathes for twenty mile around
and there's not one yard of that ground will take me when I'm done.
Rada — was there ever a word for what I was to you?

[Verse 2 — slow again]
We buried her in April with the whole road standing there,
four men to a corner, and her sons had it arranged.
Somebody put a hand flat on my chest — kindly. Not unkindly.
So I stood down by the fence and I held the horses while it went.

[Chorus — full band]
so walk him out, walk him sound, let them see him moving clean —
a dog born in a stable is a dog, and they were right to say it.
I can shoe near anything that breathes for twenty mile around
and there's not one yard of that ground will take me when I'm done.
Rada — was there ever a word for what I was to you?

[Bridge — half-time, sparse, then huge]
maybe blood was never soil
maybe soil was never blood
maybe every gate I hung straight
was a gate I stood outside
— (violin builds, tempo doubles) —
so walk him ooon—

[Final Chorus — biggest, gang vocals on the last line]
walk him out, walk him sound, let them see him moving clean —
a dog born in a stable is a dog, and they were right to say it.
I can shoe near anything that breathes for twenty mile around,
and the ground I keep level is a ground I don't go under.
(gang) and I'd have said the same.
and I'd have said the same.
