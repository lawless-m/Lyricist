[Verse 1 — slow, intimate, solo violin, rubato]
The roses want deadheading. I have said it all my life:
you take the head that's finished so the plant can spend elsewhere.
Forty year I kept this garden and I said it about people,
with a full plate in my belly and two hands that still worked.

[Pre-chorus — building, accordion swells in]
and the fiddle keeps the spent notes
that my hands would have cut down

[Chorus — full band, dragging, then lifting]
so cut it low, cut it kind, let the finished heads fall on the path,
we take the bloom that's had its summer so the young ones get the light.
I fed him forty summers and I counted every plate;
he lifts me up the stairs at night and keeps no ledger of the weight.
Emil — do you know what I called you, when I still had my own hands?

[Verse 2 — slow again]
My brother Emil never earned. Fever took his legs at four.
The house fed him, and I fed him, and I grudged him every year.
He is sixty-three this April and he shaves me in the morning,
and he hums the whole way through it, and he is careful round my throat.

[Chorus — full band]
so cut it low, cut it kind, let the finished heads fall on the path,
we take the bloom that's had its summer so the young ones get the light.
I fed him forty summers and I counted every plate;
he lifts me up the stairs at night and keeps no ledger of the weight.
Emil — do you know what I called you, when I still had my own hands?

[Bridge — half-time, sparse, then huge]
maybe worth was never work
maybe worth was never yield
maybe every hand that takes the knife
believes it's tending, not the field
— (violin builds, tempo doubles) —
so let it grooow—

[Final Chorus — biggest, gang vocals on the last line]
so cut it low, cut it kind, let the finished heads fall where they fall,
I have been out in this garden since before the house changed hands.
I counted every plate I fed him; he has not counted one —
and he was right, and I was late, and the garden carried on.
(gang) I was the bloom I cut.
I was the bloom I cut.
