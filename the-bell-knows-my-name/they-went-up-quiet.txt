[Verse 1 — slow, intimate, solo violin, rubato]
Seven of them, and the lorry came at eight, and the man was very decent about it.
I've had four since they were foaled. The grey one I sat up with, the whole of one night.
They load if I ask them. That's the thing about them. They load because I'm the one asking.
Anca went in November. The horses were fine. The horses have been fine all the way through.

[Pre-chorus — building, accordion swells in]
and the fiddle takes it off me
because somebody has to hold it

[Chorus — full band]
so load them slow, load them quiet, keep your hand where they can see it —
the horses can't cry. If they could, they would.
I can, and I haven't, and I'm the one they follow up the ramp.
And what was I meant to do with seven horses?

[Verse 2 — slow again]
Anca did the feeds. Anca did the feet. Anca had a way with the bad one.
I do the feeds now. The bad one is fine with me. That's the part I can't —
The man asked if they had names. I said not really.
They have all got names. I have called every one of them by name this morning.

[Chorus — full band]
so load them slow, load them quiet, keep your hand where they can see it —
the horses can't cry. If they could, they would.
I can, and I haven't, and I'm the one they follow up the ramp.
And what was I meant to do with seven horses?

[Bridge — half-time, sparse, then huge]
maybe grief was never a face
maybe a face was never the proof
maybe the reason they stand like that
is the reason I stand like this
— (violin builds, tempo doubles) —
so load them ooon—

[Final Chorus — biggest, gang vocals on the last line]
load them slow, load them quiet, keep your hand where they can see it —
the horses can't cry. If they could, they would.
I can, and I haven't, and the lorry is at the end of the lane.
And what was I meant to do — what was I meant to do —
(gang) they went up quiet.
they went up quiet.
