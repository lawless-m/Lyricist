[Verse 1 — slow, intimate, solo violin, rubato]
The dogs go off at everything. Fox, wind, a bag on the wire, the moon.
Four of them, and when one starts the other three are obliged.
I used to get up. I'd stand in the door with the lamp and see nothing, and go back.
Twenty years of nothing. You stop getting up. Anybody would.

[Pre-chorus — building, accordion swells in]
and the fiddle joins in with them
and it doesn't know why either

[Chorus — full band]
so keep it low, keep it listening, let the lamp stay where it is —
they only ever bark at what they don't know.
She walked past every one of them and not one of them woke.
were you counting on that, or did it just work out?

[Verse 2 — slow again]
Her boots were by the step at ten and they were not there at six.
Nothing gone but her coat and the tin with her mother's rings in it.
Sixteen. She'd have come quiet down the track, past all four kennels, in the dark.
And the dogs had a good night. Best night's sleep they'd had in years.

[Chorus — full band]
so keep it low, keep it listening, let the lamp stay where it is —
they only ever bark at what they don't know.
She walked past every one of them and not one of them woke.
were you counting on that, or did it just work out?

[Bridge — half-time, sparse, then huge]
maybe a guard was never a fence
maybe a fence was never a want
maybe everything I built to keep the strangers out
kept nothing in at all
— (violin builds, tempo doubles) —
so let them baaark—

[Final Chorus — biggest, gang vocals on the last line]
keep it low, keep it listening, let the lamp stay where it is —
they only ever bark at what they don't know,
and I taught them that myself, and I fed them for it,
and they have not woken me once since the night I needed it.
(gang) they only bark at strangers.
they only bark at strangers.
