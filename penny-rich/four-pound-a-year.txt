[Verse 1 — banjo-vaudeville, older and sillier, closer to the tent show. Bright warm female lead vocal, quick and comic.]
They all know what I am. I'm the one that knows what everything come off of.
Church sale, boot fair, the reduced trolley at half four on a Sunday.
Sandra calls me before she buys a kettle. Sandra has called me about a kettle.
And I do love it. I'll not pretend I don't.

[Pre-chorus — mandolin chop tightens, bass pushes, harmony steps up behind]
But the boots were sixty pound in two thousand and one
and I paid the sixty pound and I paid it happy

[Chorus — full band, close three-part harmony in from the second line]
So say I like a bargain.
Say I like a bargain, say I'd walk a mile for tuppence, say it's how I'm made.
Boots were sixty pound and they've been soled four times. That's four pound a year.
She does love a bargain.
She has always loved a bargain.

[Verse 2 — the working, fast and specific]
Cheap boots is forty pound and you'll have three pair by the time I've had one.
That's a hundred and twenty, and wet feet in the middle of all of it.
Same with a coat, a mattress, a pan, and a man who'll come and do your guttering.
Everything else in this house come off a table in a car park and I'd do it again.
It's not the price. It's the price divided by the years, and nobody does that bit.
Sandra says she wishes she had my luck at a boot fair.
Sandra buys three of everything. Sandra has got three kettles.

[Chorus — identical words, harmony wider]
So say I like a bargain.
Say I like a bargain, say I'd walk a mile for tuppence, say it's how I'm made.
Boots were sixty pound and they've been soled four times. That's four pound a year.
She does love a bargain.
She has always loved a bargain.

[The plain line and the break — band stops dead, one flat line, no vibrato, no repeat]
The boots will see me out. I did that sum an' all.

[Instrumental break — banjo, fiddle, mandolin trading eight bars each, fast and comic, and gone]

[Final chorus — biggest, full three-part close harmony all the way through, everybody singing the wrong version with her]
So say I like a bargain.
Say I like a bargain, say I'd walk a mile for tuppence, say it's how I'm made.
Boots were sixty pound and they've been soled four times. That's four pound a year.
She does love a bargain.
She'll die loving a bargain.

[Hard bluegrass tag — everybody hits the last note together and stops dead, no fade]
