[Verse 1 — slow mountain ballad, waltz time, dobro and fingerpicked guitar, no banjo roll, upright bass, distant fiddle, plain warm female voice recorded close.]
Cyril went on the Monday and there wasn't anybody left to ring.
Sister's gone. Wife's gone. There's a nephew somewhere out past Leeds.
So I rang round instead. Took me the best part of an afternoon and a notebook.
Fifty-three came in the end.

[Pre-chorus — bass steps up, close family harmony behind]
Chapel at eleven, cars at half ten
because eleven gives them the morning off and not the day

[Chorus — full band, close three-part harmony in from the second line]
So say the man did it.
Say the man did it, say it all went lovely, say they do a lovely job.
You want the hymn they can all sing. Not the one he liked. The one they know.
That funeral director's marvellous.
That funeral director has always been marvellous.

[Verse 2 — the working, unhurried but specific]
Ham for sixty, not for forty, because people who don't come still come after.
Tea urn on before the cars get back, not after, or you lose them to the car park.
Sandwiches cut in four. Four, not two. They'll take one and they'll take another.
Order of service on the pale card, because the white shows every thumb.
And you put the nephew at the front whether he earned it or he didn't.
The man from the firm shook my hand at the gate and said we'd done him proud.
We had. We did do him proud. That part's true.

[Chorus — identical words, harmony wider]
